ALM Stress Tester

Where do funding gaps open when customers pay late or funding leaves, and how long does the liquid buffer last?

Prototype: the functions below exist and run on synthetic data. The workbook has not been independently validated and is not on sale. What the status labels mean.

Scope

What it does, and what it does not.

  • Applies six stress scenarios to one cash-flow ladder and compares them side by side.
  • Reports, for each scenario, the liquid buffer after haircut, the lowest cumulative position, the number of time buckets survived and the extra buffer needed to reach the policy horizon.
  • Reports the amount of financing inflows delayed and an illustrative funding cost of that delay, separately from the liquidity gap.
  • Shows INCOMPLETE, never a pass, when a data check fails.

This version covers liquidity stress only. It does not measure the profit-rate sensitivity of earnings or economic value, displaced commercial risk, or any regulatory ratio.

Inputs

What you enter.

Contractual inflows

Six classes (retail, corporate and public-sector Murabaha receivables, Ijara rentals, Sukuk held, other) across eight time buckets from 0-1M to over 5Y, in one currency and unit.

Contractual outflows

Six classes (current accounts, profit-sharing investment accounts, term and Wakala deposits, wholesale and commodity Murabaha funding, Sukuk issued, operating and other) across the same buckets.

Buffer and commitments

Cash and central bank balances, liquid Sukuk at market value, undrawn financing commitments.

Policy inputs

Funding cost per year, the policy survival horizon as a bucket number, and the longest delay counted in the cost of delay.

Scenario library

Eleven rates for each of six scenarios: four delay rates, a non-payment rate, three accelerated-withdrawal rates, a wholesale rollover rate, a commitment drawdown rate and a Sukuk haircut. The sixth scenario is the user's own.

Method

How each figure is calculated.

For a financing class with contractual inflow Cb in bucket b, non-payment rate l and delay rate d, the stressed inflow is:

Inflow(1) = C(1) x (1 - l) x (1 - d) Inflow(b) = C(b) x (1 - l) x (1 - d) + C(b-1) x (1 - l) x d for b = 2 to 7 Inflow(8) = C(8) x (1 - l) + C(7) x (1 - l) x d

The last line is an end-of-ladder convention: the model does not track cash beyond the final bucket, so nothing is delayed out of it. Figures in that bucket should not be read as a liquidity forecast.

For a funding class with accelerated-withdrawal rate w, the stated share of all later outflows is pulled into the first bucket:

Outflow(1) = O(1) + w x [O(2) + ... + O(8)] Outflow(b) = O(b) x (1 - w) for b > 1

Maturing wholesale funding is entered gross. The scenario assumes a stated share is renewed, and only the remainder is counted as a net outflow. Renewal is an assumption about future funding, not a contractual right, so the gross maturities remain the exposure. A stated share of undrawn commitments is drawn in the first bucket. The buffer is cash plus liquid Sukuk after the scenario haircut.

Buffer = Cash + Sukuk x (1 - haircut) Position(b) = Buffer + sum of net cash flows from bucket 1 to b Buckets survived = number of completed buckets before the first negative bucket-end position Delayed(b) = sum over financing classes of C(b) x (1 - l) x d, for b = 1 to 7 Cost of delay = sum over b of Delayed(b) x funding cost per year x min(cap, m(b+1) - m(b))

Here m(b) is the midpoint of bucket b in years and the cap is also in years. Amounts assumed unpaid are excluded before the delay is applied. One term from the reference example: in the payment-delay scenario 34.452 is delayed out of the first bucket, the funding cost is 4.5% a year and the midpoints are 0.125 years apart, giving 34.452 x 0.045 x 0.125 = 0.194. A result of zero buckets survived means the first bucket ends negative. It does not say on which day liquidity ran out.

The cost of delay is an illustrative incremental funding cost under the entered rate and timing. It is not the bank's actual cost of funds or an accounting expense. The model assumes none of it is recovered from the customer. What an institution may recover for late payment depends on its contracts, its governing Sharia standard and the applicable law, and is not modelled.

Limitations

When not to rely on it.

  • Delays move cash by one bucket only. Buckets have unequal lengths, so one bucket is a longer delay at the far end than at the near end.
  • Placement of accounts without a contractual maturity is the user's assumption.
  • No new business, no management actions and no asset sales beyond the buffer are modelled.
  • Scenario parameters are illustrative. They are not calibrated to any institution, market or regulatory requirement.
  • Profit-sharing investment accounts are treated as funding that can leave. Displaced commercial risk on their returns is not modelled.
