The equations

Returns are computed from prices Pt as logarithmic or simple returns:

$$\begin{gathered}r_t=\ln\frac{P_t}{P_{t-1}}\\[6pt] r_t=\frac{P_t}{P_{t-1}}-1.\end{gathered}$$

At confidence level p, value at risk is the loss exceeded with probability 1 − p, and expected shortfall is the average loss beyond it, where q1−p is the (1 − p) quantile of returns:

$$\begin{gathered}\mathrm{VaR}_p=-q_{1-p},\\[6pt] \mathrm{ES}_p=-\,\mathbb{E}\left[\,r\mid r\le q_{1-p}\right].\end{gathered}$$

Historical model: the empirical quantile of the n returns, and the average of the k returns at or below it.

$$\begin{gathered}\mathrm{VaR}^{H}_p=-\hat q_{1-p},\\[6pt] \mathrm{ES}^{H}_p=-\frac{1}{k}\sum_{r_t\le\hat q_{1-p}} r_t.\end{gathered}$$

Normal model, with the sample mean μ̂ and standard deviation σ̂, where Φ and φ are the standard normal distribution and density:

$$\begin{gathered}\mathrm{VaR}^{N}_p=-\big(\hat\mu+\hat\sigma\,\Phi^{-1}(1-p)\big),\\[6pt] \mathrm{ES}^{N}_p=-\hat\mu+\hat\sigma\,\frac{\varphi\big(\Phi^{-1}(1-p)\big)}{1-p}.\end{gathered}$$

Stable model, with F the distribution function of the fitted S(α, β, σ, μ); expected shortfall is finite only for α > 1:

$$\begin{gathered}\mathrm{VaR}^{S}_p=-F^{-1}(1-p),\\[6pt] \mathrm{ES}^{S}_p=\mathrm{VaR}^{S}_p+\frac{1}{1-p}\int_{-\infty}^{-\mathrm{VaR}^{S}_p}F(x)\,dx.\end{gathered}$$

The stable parameters come from McCulloch’s quantile ratios, matched to the same ratios of the stable law:

$$\begin{gathered}\nu_\alpha=\frac{x_{0.95}-x_{0.05}}{x_{0.75}-x_{0.25}},\\[6pt] \nu_\beta=\frac{x_{0.95}+x_{0.05}-2x_{0.50}}{x_{0.95}-x_{0.05}}.\end{gathered}$$

A breach is a return below −VaR. Over n returns, N breaches are counted and n(1 − p) are expected:

$$\begin{gathered}I_t=\mathbf 1\{r_t<-\mathrm{VaR}_p\},\\[6pt] N=\sum_{t=1}^{n} I_t,\\[6pt] \mathbb E[N]=n(1-p).\end{gathered}$$

Kupiec’s (1995) test compares the breach rate π = 1 − p with the observed rate N/n; under the model the statistic follows a χ² law with one degree of freedom:

$$\mathrm{LR}=-2\ln\frac{(1-\pi)^{\,n-N}\,\pi^{N}}{\big(1-\tfrac{N}{n}\big)^{n-N}\big(\tfrac{N}{n}\big)^{N}}\;\sim\;\chi^2_1 .$$

The ARMA(1,1)–GARCH(1,1) model gives each return a conditional mean μt and a conditional variance σt², both known one period ahead:

$$\begin{gathered}r_t=\underbrace{c+\phi\,r_{t-1}+\theta\,\varepsilon_{t-1}}_{\mu_t}+\varepsilon_t,\qquad \varepsilon_t=\sigma_t z_t,\\[6pt] \sigma_t^2=\omega+\alpha_1\varepsilon_{t-1}^2+\beta_1\sigma_{t-1}^2 .\end{gathered}$$

It is estimated in two steps: Gaussian quasi-maximum likelihood for the model, then maximum likelihood for a stable law fitted to the standardised residuals zt = εt/σt:

$$\begin{gathered}\hat\vartheta=\arg\max\;-\tfrac12\sum_t\Big(\ln 2\pi+\ln\sigma_t^2+\frac{\varepsilon_t^2}{\sigma_t^2}\Big),\\[6pt] (\hat\alpha,\hat\beta,\hat\sigma_z,\hat\mu_z)=\arg\max\sum_t\ln f_S(\hat z_t).\end{gathered}$$

The conditional VaR and expected shortfall scale the innovation quantile qz (normal Φ⁻¹, or the fitted stable F⁻¹) by the conditional volatility:

$$\begin{gathered}\mathrm{VaR}_{p,t}=-\big(\mu_t+\sigma_t\,q_z(1-p)\big),\\[6pt] \mathrm{ES}_{p,t}=-\mu_t+\sigma_t\,\mathrm{ES}_z(p).\end{gathered}$$

Christoffersen’s (1998) independence test compares the probability of a breach after a breach, π11, with that after a quiet day, π01, where nij counts days in state j after a day in state i; adding Kupiec’s statistic gives conditional coverage:

$$\begin{gathered}\mathrm{LR}_{\mathrm{ind}}=-2\ln\frac{(1-\hat\pi)^{n_{00}+n_{10}}\,\hat\pi^{\,n_{01}+n_{11}}}{(1-\hat\pi_{01})^{n_{00}}\hat\pi_{01}^{\,n_{01}}(1-\hat\pi_{11})^{n_{10}}\hat\pi_{11}^{\,n_{11}}}\sim\chi^2_1,\\[6pt] \mathrm{LR}_{\mathrm{cc}}=\mathrm{LR}_{\mathrm{uc}}+\mathrm{LR}_{\mathrm{ind}}\sim\chi^2_2 .\end{gathered}$$

Normality is tested with Jarque–Bera, from the skewness S and excess kurtosis K, also against a χ² law, with two degrees of freedom:

$$\mathrm{JB}=\frac{n}{6}\Big(S^2+\frac{K^2}{4}\Big)\;\sim\;\chi^2_2 .$$

How it is computed

  • Returns. Prices become logarithmic returns, ln(Pt/Pt−1), or simple returns if you choose. Rows that are missing or not numeric are skipped, and dates in reverse order are put in time order.
  • Normality. The Jarque–Bera statistic, n/6 · (S² + K²/4), combines skewness S and excess kurtosis K; its p-value comes from the χ² law with two degrees of freedom.
  • Stable fit. McCulloch’s (1986) quantile method matches the 5, 25, 50, 75 and 95% quantiles of the data with those of S(α, β, σ, μ) in the S1 parameterisation used in the book. The reference table was computed with the same numerics as the stable-law page.
  • Risk. Value at risk is the loss exceeded with probability 1 − p; expected shortfall is the average loss beyond it. The historical figures use the data alone, the normal ones the sample mean and standard deviation, and the stable ones the fitted law, with expected shortfall integrated numerically. Both are for one period and expressed as a percentage of the position.
