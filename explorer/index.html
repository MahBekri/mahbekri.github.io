α-Stable Distributions

Interactive

Move α, β, σ and μ and see at once how the stable law S(α, β, σ, μ) changes shape, and how far its tails reach beyond the Gaussian.

Density and distribution function

α ≤ 1: the mean does not exist, so expected shortfall is infinite.1 < α < 2: the mean exists but the variance is infinite.α = 2: the Gaussian case, with variance 2σ². Near α = 1 with β ≠ 0, the S1 parameterisation moves the whole distribution by about −βσ tan(πα/2), so the curve can leave the plotted range.

The parameters

  • α, the tail index, between 0 and 2: the smaller it is, the heavier the tails. α = 2 gives the Gaussian, α = 1 the Cauchy.
  • β, the skewness, between −1 and 1: positive values stretch the right tail, negative values the left.
  • σ, the scale, which sets the spread like a standard deviation; at α = 2 the standard deviation is √2·σ (the book writes σ as γ).
  • μ, the location (the book writes it δ).

The equations

A stable law is defined by its characteristic function. In the S1 parameterisation used here and in the book:

$$\begin{aligned}\varphi(u)&=\mathbb{E}\,e^{iuX}\\&=\exp\!\Big(i\mu u-|\sigma u|^{\alpha}\big[1-i\beta\operatorname{sign}(u)\tan\tfrac{\pi\alpha}{2}\big]\Big),\ \ \alpha\neq1,\\&=\exp\!\Big(i\mu u-\sigma|u|\big[1+i\beta\tfrac{2}{\pi}\operatorname{sign}(u)\ln|u|\big]\Big),\ \ \alpha=1.\end{aligned}$$

The density (upper figure) and the distribution function (lower figure) follow by inverting it:

$$f(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{Re}\!\big[e^{-iux}\,\varphi(u)\big]\,du,$$ $$F(x)=\mathbb{P}(X\le x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}f(t)\,dt.$$

Only three cases have a closed form: the Gaussian, the Cauchy and the Lévy laws.

$$\alpha=2:\quad X\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu,\,2\sigma^{2}),$$ $$\alpha=1,\ \beta=0:\quad f(x)=\frac{\sigma}{\pi\,[\sigma^{2}+(x-\mu)^{2}]},$$ $$\alpha=\tfrac12,\ \beta=1:\quad f(x)=\sqrt{\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}}\;\frac{e^{-\sigma/(2(x-\mu))}}{(x-\mu)^{3/2}},\quad x>\mu.$$

For α < 2 the tails decay as a power of x, not exponentially, which is why the density in the upper figure stays far above the Gaussian. As x → ∞:

$$\mathbb{P}(X>x)\sim C_{\alpha}\,(1+\beta)\,\sigma^{\alpha}\,x^{-\alpha},$$ $$f(x)\sim \alpha\,C_{\alpha}\,(1+\beta)\,\sigma^{\alpha}\,x^{-\alpha-1},\qquad C_{\alpha}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)\,\sin(\pi\alpha/2)}{\pi},$$

and the same holds in the left tail with 1 − β in place of 1 + β. Consequently, moments exist only below α:

$$\mathbb{E}\,|X|^{p}<\infty\iff p<\alpha\quad(\alpha<2),$$

so the mean, and with it expected shortfall, is finite only for α > 1, and the variance only for α = 2.

How it is computed

The figures follow the S1 parameterisation used in the book and in MATLAB. Because the Fourier integral above oscillates, they are computed in your browser from Nolan’s (1997) integral representation, with Gauss–Legendre quadrature, and match SciPy to five significant digits for the density and to 10⁻⁵ for the distribution function. The dashed curves are the Gaussian case, α = 2, with the same σ and μ.
