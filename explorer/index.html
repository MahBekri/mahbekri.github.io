Stable law explorer

Interactive

Move α, β, σ and μ and see at once how the stable law S(α, β, σ, μ) changes shape, and how far its tails reach beyond the Gaussian.

The density

α ≤ 1: the mean does not exist, so expected shortfall is infinite.1 < α < 2: the mean exists but the variance is infinite.α = 2: the Gaussian case, with variance 2σ². Near α = 1 with β ≠ 0, the S1 parameterisation moves the whole distribution by about −βσ tan(πα/2), so the curve can leave the plotted range.

The parameters

  • α, the tail index, between 0 and 2: the smaller it is, the heavier the tails. α = 2 gives the Gaussian, α = 1 the Cauchy.
  • β, the skewness, between −1 and 1: positive values stretch the right tail, negative values the left.
  • σ, the scale, which plays the role of the standard deviation (the book writes it γ).
  • μ, the location (the book writes it δ).

How it is computed

The density follows the S1 parameterisation used in the book and in MATLAB. It is computed in your browser from Nolan’s integral representation with Gauss–Legendre quadrature, and matches SciPy to five significant digits. The dashed curve is the Gaussian case, α = 2, with the same σ and μ, whose variance is 2σ².
