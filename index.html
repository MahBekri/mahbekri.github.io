About

I trained as a physicist at the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT) before moving into financial econometrics. My doctorate in Economics and Finance, supervised by Svetlozar Rachev, Wolf-Dieter Heller and Frank Fabozzi, applied stable and tempered stable distributions to portfolio risk. I then held a postdoctoral position at KIT's Institute of Economic Theory and Statistics.

I joined the Islamic Development Bank Group through its Young Professionals Program and now work as a Research Economist at the IsDB Institute. My research brings heavy-tailed modelling and coherent risk measures to Islamic finance, from Sharia-screened equity portfolios to hedging and asset-liability management. With jurist co-authors I also write on the ethics of new commercial practices in Islamic law.

Short bio

Mahmoud Bekri is a Research Economist at the Islamic Development Bank Institute (IsDBI) in Jeddah. Trained as a physicist at the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT), Bekri completed a PhD in Economics and Finance there under Svetlozar Rachev, Wolf-Dieter Heller and Frank Fabozzi, followed by postdoctoral research. Bekri’s work applies heavy-tailed models and coherent risk measures to Islamic finance, from Sharia-screened portfolios to hedging, and is set out in the book Heavy Tails and Coherent Risk Measures in Islamic Finance (IsDBI, 2021).

Ebook

Heavy Tails and Coherent Risk Measures in Islamic Finance (Islamic Development Bank Institute, Jeddah, 2021; 170 pp.; ISBN 978-603-8306-06-2) is a hands-on introduction to heavy-tailed distributions and coherent risk measures, written for academics, practitioners and students of Islamic finance.

Financial returns make extreme moves far more often than the normal distribution allows. The book shows how stable laws, copulas and coherent measures such as Average Value at Risk capture that tail risk, and how to apply them to Sharia-compliant portfolios through worked examples.

It is organised in four parts:

  1. An overview of Islamic finance: its sources, core principles and guidelines.
  2. Probability theory, time series modelling, heavy-tailed and stable distributions, and dependence structures.
  3. Applying these methods to risk management, with an emphasis on market risk.
  4. Portfolio selection and optimisation, and performance measurement.
