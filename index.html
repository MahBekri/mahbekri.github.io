Mahmoud Bekri

Research Economist · Islamic Development Bank Institute, Jeddah

I study what happens in the tails of financial returns, where the Gaussian model says losses should almost never occur and markets say otherwise, and what that means for Islamic finance.

About

I trained as a physicist at the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT) before moving into financial econometrics. My doctorate in Economics and Finance, supervised by Svetlozar Rachev and Frank Fabozzi, applied stable and tempered stable distributions to portfolio risk. I then held a postdoctoral position at KIT's Institute of Economic Theory and Statistics.

I joined the Islamic Development Bank Group through its Young Professionals Program and now work as a Research Economist at the IsDB Institute. My research brings heavy-tailed modelling and coherent risk measures to Islamic finance, from Sharia-screened equity portfolios to hedging and asset-liability management. With jurist co-authors I also write on the ethics of new commercial practices in Islamic law.
